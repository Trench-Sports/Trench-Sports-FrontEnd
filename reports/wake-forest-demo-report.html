Session Summary

The Wake Forest demo captured 24 power-mode rounds — 4,097 bag contacts over roughly nine minutes of active hitting — split evenly across three athletes. Athlete 1 was the most efficient per swing, generating the group's highest per-contact intensity with the most centered strike cluster. Athlete 2 was the motor of the group: nearly double A1's contact volume and the fastest hands (23.7 contacts/sec peak), but per-hit quality dropped sharply at top speed. Athlete 3 split the difference, pairing above-average intensity with high volume, though long mid-round pauses point to pacing as the next focus. The headline for the staff: every athlete repeatedly maxed out the force sensor — these are strong hitters, and a calibrated rig at the next visit will produce true newton-scale numbers.

Appendix — Reading the Results

What every metric in this report measures, and how to use it
Reference
Page 5 of 5
Contacts / Events count
Each distinct impact the bag registers. One swing that lands clean counts once, even when it lights up several sensor cells at the same instant.
Strength Index 0–225 in this demo
Trench's per-hit intensity score, computed from how hard and how sharply each impact loads the sensor grid. Higher is harder. The "avg" is weighted across every contact, so high-volume rounds count proportionally.
Peak Reading volts (mV)
The single largest raw sensor reading in a round. The demo rig saturates at ≈3.3 V — every athlete pinned it, so peaks shown are floors, not ceilings. Newton conversion requires a calibrated rig.
Impulse (Avg) volts
Average sensor voltage sustained across contacts — a proxy for how much energy each swing transfers into the bag, not just the instantaneous spike.
Rise / Decay Time ms
How fast force builds into the bag (rise) and how fast it releases (decay). Short rise = sharp, snappy contact. Long rise or decay usually means pushing or dragging through the bag rather than striking it.
Impact Angle degrees, −180° to +180°
Orientation of each impact across the bag face, with 0° square to the bag. The mean shows the dominant strike direction; the shaded band on the dial shows spread (±1 std). A tight band = repeatable barrel delivery.
Contact Map / Heatmap 8 cols × 12 rows
Where contact energy landed on the bag face (log scale — the brightest cells absorbed the most cumulative energy). Row 1 is the top of the bag, column 1 the left edge. Corner cells can include mount/lean artifacts.
Center of Mass grid coordinates
The energy-weighted "average" landing spot of all contacts, marked ○ on the map. Grid center is column 4.5, row 6.5 — distance from center is a one-number read on strike placement.
Cadence hits per second (Hz)
Contact rate over the active round. Power rounds run slower by design; the value matters most when compared against the strength index it was achieved at — speed that holds intensity is the goal.
Inter-Event Interval & Longest Pause ms / s
Average gap between consecutive contacts, and the single longest gap in a round. Big pauses mid-round flag resets, instruction breaks, or fatigue — useful context before judging a low cadence number.
Most-Contacted Cell row, column
The single grid cell hit most in a round. When this sits far from the center of mass, contact is spread or bimodal rather than clustered.
Session Duration mm:ss
Active recording time per round, from first to last registered event. Cumulative time per athlete appears in their page header.

Data Quality Notes — This Demo

Sensor Saturation Peak readings of ≈3,280–3,300 mV across rounds indicate the ADC ceiling was reached repeatedly. Relative comparisons (strength index, impulse averages) remain valid; absolute force in newtons requires the calibrated production rig.
De-Identified Data Sessions were recorded without athlete, program, or team IDs and assigned to Athletes 1–3 by rotation order. Eight rounds per athlete, June 9, 2026, all in Power mode. Corner-cell dwell values (esp. row 1, col 1) include probable mount-contact artifacts and are damped by the log scale on the maps.
